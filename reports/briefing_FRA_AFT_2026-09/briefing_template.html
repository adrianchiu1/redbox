Bottom line

  1. The interest bill is no longer a rate story, it is a stock story. The fixed-rate OAT stock still carries an average coupon of {{avg_coupon_fixed_stock_2026-09-10_pct}}% against a marginal 10-year yield near 4.2–4.4%. In 2027 alone the AFT refinances €{{redemptions_2027_eur_bn_round}}bn of maturing paper (bills included) and adds €140–180bn of net supply, all at the marginal rate. On the register alone that pushes general-government interest from 2.2% of GDP (2025) to {{cur_2030_pct}}% in 2030 and {{cur_2035_pct}}% in 2035 with no political shock at all, which is within a few billion of the EC Debt Sustainability Monitor path already in the strict file.
  2. Politics adds €{{lp_2030_gap}}–{{rup_2030_gap}}bn a year by 2030 and €{{lp_2035_gap}}–{{rup_2035_gap}}bn by 2035 in the Le Pen configurations, of which roughly two-thirds is the spread and one-third the primary balance. A Philippe majority saves only €{{pm_2035_gap_abs}}bn a year by 2035: consolidation cannot outrun the coupon roll. The asymmetry is the point for a holder: the downside cases are far larger than the upside case.
  3. The wrong-way loop is real but slow. Feeding the spread back through the deficit into issuance adds under €{{lp_2035_interaction_round}}bn a year by 2035 even in the Le Pen-majority case. The loop is mostly a market-pricing loop (spread, ratings, index eligibility), not a fiscal-arithmetic loop, at least out to 2030. That is where to press the AFT: what stops the pricing loop, not the arithmetic.
  4. The stock is shortening at the margin, not yet in aggregate. Average residual maturity of the register peaked at 8.5 years in 2022 and is {{avg_residual_maturity_2025-12-31_all_years}} years at end-2025 (the AFT's own figure: 8 years 142 days at 31 August 2026). Maturity at issue of fixed-rate paper has fallen from {{avg_maturity_at_issue_fixed_bullet_2021_years}} years (2021) to {{avg_maturity_at_issue_fixed_bullet_2026ytd_years}} years (2026 to date), and 32% of the stock matures inside three years: €{{redemptions_2026_eur_bn}}bn in the rest of 2026, €{{redemptions_2027_eur_bn}}bn in 2027, €{{redemptions_2028_eur_bn}}bn in 2028.
  5. Rate sensitivity sits with holders who can leave. The OAT stock has a modified duration of {{dur_mod}} years and loses about €{{dur_loss100}}bn of market value per 100 bp. Non-residents hold 57.5% of negotiable debt, the Eurosystem has run its French holdings down from €546bn to €488bn in six months and is still shrinking, and French life insurers (9.6%) are absorbing supply on record inflows that reverse when rates rise. The marginal buyer of 2027–2028 is not the buyer of 2015–2021.

1Where the stock stands

The Redbox register holds every OAT, BTAN, OATi, OAT€i and BTF the AFT has listed since January 1999: 1,603 securities, positions rolled back from the AFT's encours pages through its auction histories, with the office's own daily indexation coefficients for the 35 linkers (D-S10-007). At the 10 September 2026 snapshot the register carries €{{stock_2026-09-10_total_nominal_eur_bn_round}}bn nominal, €{{stock_2026-09-10_total_uplifted_eur_bn_round}}bn once linkers are uplifted: bills €{{stock_2026-09-10_bill_nominal_eur_bn_round}}bn, fixed-rate €{{stock_2026-09-10_fixed_bullet_nominal_eur_bn_round}}bn, linkers €{{stock_2026-09-10_inflation_linked_nominal_eur_bn_round}}bn nominal (€{{stock_2026-09-10_inflation_linked_uplifted_eur_bn_round}}bn uplifted). The AFT's own total was €2,903.8bn at 31 August ext. The register runs 1–4% above the AFT on the fixed lines because the AFT publishes buybacks only in aggregate, so lines near maturity are overstated; Σ BTF reproduced the AFT total to the euro over 2009–2020 and linkers are within 3%.

Two features of the composition matter for what follows. First, the bill stock: BTF outstanding was €107bn at end-2019, €{{btf_2025_bn}}bn at end-2025, and the AFT plans to hold it near €215–220bn through 2026 ext. A bill stock of that size is a rolling €200bn+ of one-year-or-less funding at the €STR-linked rate, which the engine below prices at the short end of the OAT curve. Second, linkers: 8% of the stock at nominal, 11% uplifted. Their uplift accrues as interest in the national accounts (ESA 2010 §4.46), so with inflation at 2% they contribute roughly €6bn a year to D.41 before any coupon; in 2022–2023 that was €5–8bn (Figure 8).

2The interest bill and the wrong-way loop

Where it has come from

General-government interest (COFOG 01.7, the package's GF01_7) fell from 3.8% of GDP in 1996 to 1.4% in 2020, on a falling average coupon rather than a falling stock: the stock rose the whole time. It was 2.0% in 2024 (€{{gf01_7_2024_eur_bn}}bn) and about {{gf01_7_2025_pct_gdp}}% in 2025 (€{{gf01_7_2025_eur_bn}}bn, the AMECO-stitched value; the Eurostat outturn arrives in October). Net interest was already 1.8% of GDP in 2024 against a primary deficit of 3.9%, so the primary balance, not interest, made the 2024 deficit. That ordering is about to flip.

Why it rises whatever happens next

The register makes the mechanism concrete. The nominal-weighted average coupon on the fixed-rate stock bottomed at {{avg_coupon_fixed_stock_2022-12-31_pct}}% at end-2022, was {{avg_coupon_fixed_stock_2025-12-31_pct}}% at end-2025 and is {{avg_coupon_fixed_stock_2026-09-10_pct}}% at the September snapshot (Figure 6). The lines maturing in 2027–2028 carry coupons of 0–1% (the 0% November 2029/2030/2031 and 0.5–1.25% lines of 2019–2022 issuance); they are replaced at 3.5–4.4%. The AFT's own new-issuance rate has climbed from 3.45% in February 2026 to 4.23% at the 3 September auction ext. Nothing a government does in 2027 changes the coupons on the €{{redemptions_2027_eur_bn}}bn that matures that year; policy only changes the rate on the replacement and the size of the addition.

The loop, priced

Redbox now rolls the register forward (module ggfiscal.debt.simulate, new in this session). Each year the engine matures what the register says matures, pays the coupons it says are owed, accrues uplift on the linkers at 2%, prices the bill stock at the short end, finances the general-government deficit (primary balance declared, interest endogenous) at the marginal OAT yield in the AFT's 2025 mix of tenors, and feeds the interest back into next year's deficit. The Bund curve is held flat at today's levels (3-month 2.5%, 10-year 3.4%); everything that differs between scenarios is the primary balance, the spread, the bill stock and the tenor mix. The current path is not the government's trajectory, which promises 3% by 2029; it is consolidation at 0.4 pp of GDP a year, roughly what has been delivered on average since the EDP opened, with a spread easing from 85 bp to 70 bp. Every number is declared in the assumptions table in section 8.

How big is the loop? Figure 9 splits each scenario's 2030 interest gap to the current path into the spread (re-run the scenario with its spread but the current path's primary balance), the volume (its primary balance and issuance strategy with the current path's spread), and the interaction, which is the loop proper: the extra issuance that the extra interest itself requires, priced at the wider spread. In the Le Pen-majority case the 2030 gap is €{{lp_2030_gap}}bn, of which €{{lp_2030_spread}}bn is spread, €{{lp_2030_volume}}bn is volume and the interaction is under €1bn; by 2035 the gap is €{{lp_2035_gap}}bn with €{{lp_2035_interaction}}bn of interaction. The arithmetic loop is small on a ten-year view because interest is a flow of a few per cent of a stock that turns over slowly. The pricing loop is what moves quickly, and it has already moved: the spread's annual average has risen every year since 2021 ({{oat_bund_spread_2021_avg_bp}} → {{oat_bund_spread_2025_avg_bp}} bp) and is near 90 bp now, the widest since 2012, with three downgrades in 2025 (Fitch, S&P, DBRS) and negative outlooks at Moody's and Scope ext.

What to take into the room. The AFT will say, correctly, that the average cost of the stock adjusts slowly (3.16% on the OAT stock in 2025 by its own measure ext) and that an 8.4-year average maturity buys time. Both are true and both are why the current path already gets to 3.5% of GDP by 2030: slow adjustment works against France now that the marginal rate is above the average. The question that matters is not the level of the spread but whether anything below the ECB caps it. Press on the three exits: the TPI conditions (France is in an EDP and off its path, so eligibility is not automatic), the index and ratings floor (a fourth agency below AA- changes some mandates), and what the AFT itself can do on volume (buybacks, tenor, the bill stock) when the spread is the problem rather than the curve.

To ask the AFT on the interest bill

  1. What average cost on the stock and what interest charge does the PLF 2027 assume for 2027 and 2028, and at what 10-year rate? Our register-based current path gives €{{cur_2027_bn}}bn and €{{cur_2028_bn}}bn of general-government interest.
  2. How much of the 2027 redemptions has already been bought back, line by line? The register carries €{{redemptions_2027_eur_bn}}bn maturing in 2027 gross of buybacks; the AFT's 2026 programme implied €175.8bn of medium/long-term redemptions for 2026.
  3. Does the AFT model a spread feedback in its own debt-service projections, or hold the spread at the current forward?

3The shortening stock

The register's average residual maturity rose from 6.5 years (2009) to a peak of 8.5 years at end-2022 as the AFT sold 30- and 50-year paper into negative real yields; it is {{avg_residual_maturity_2025-12-31_all_years}} years at end-2025 and the AFT reports 8 years 142 days at 31 August 2026, down from 8 years 180 days in June ext. The aggregate has barely moved. What has moved is the flow: the average maturity at issue of fixed-rate paper was {{avg_maturity_at_issue_fixed_bullet_2021_years}} years in 2021, {{avg_maturity_at_issue_fixed_bullet_2024_years}} in 2024, {{avg_maturity_at_issue_fixed_bullet_2025_years}} in 2025 and {{avg_maturity_at_issue_fixed_bullet_2026ytd_years}} so far in 2026 (Figure 10, right). The 20–30 and 30+ buckets that took 15% of gross issuance in 2021 took 10% in 2025 (Figure 11).

Three readings of this, and the AFT will offer the first. (i) It is demand-led: the long end has lost the Dutch and German pension bid and the Japanese carry bid, so the AFT issues where the book is, which is 3–10 years. (ii) It is cost-led: with a curve that steepened 2024–2026 and a spread that is largest at the long end, the AFT is paying up least at 5–7 years and the maturity mix follows. (iii) It is a choice about roll-over risk that the AFT would rather not make explicit: every year of average maturity given up raises the share of the stock that reprices inside the next presidential term. On the register, 32% of the stock matures within three years, and the 2027 redemption year is the heaviest on record. In the Le Pen scenarios the engine assumes the AFT does what debt offices under pressure do, which is lean on bills (+€45–85bn) and shorten the tenor mix by one to three years; that is cheaper in year one and is the mechanism by which a spread shock in 2028 becomes a permanent feature of the average cost by 2032.

The bill stock deserves its own line. BTF outstanding was 5.8% of the stock in 2019, {{bill_share_of_stock_2025_pct}}% at end-2025, and the AFT's plan holds it near €215–220bn through 2026. At 2.5–2.7% that costs €5–6bn a year and is the one part of the stock that reprices to the ECB within a year: a 100 bp policy move is €2bn of annual interest with no lag, and the same figure is what a 40 bp widening of the BTF spread to €STR would cost in a stress.

To ask the AFT on maturity

  1. Is the fall in maturity at issue a demand observation or a policy? What would bring the 20–30 year bucket back to 15% of issuance, and is there an average-maturity floor the AFT would defend?
  2. The 2027 redemption wall: how much of it does the AFT intend to pre-fund in Q4 2026 and how much to buy back, and does the PLF 2027 programme anticipate more or less than the 2026 €310bn?
  3. Under a spread shock, is the bill stock the shock absorber (as in 2008–2009, when it doubled to €214bn), or is there a ceiling?

Three holder groups, three different sensitivities

The Eurosystem is the one holder whose behaviour is known: it is leaving. French holdings under PSPP and PEPP fell from €546bn at end-2025 to €488bn at end-June 2026, about €10bn a month, with no reinvestment since PEPP ended in January 2025 ext. That is €110–120bn a year of extra net supply the market must absorb on top of the deficit, before any 2027 shock. The TPI is the counterweight, and its conditions (compliance with the EU fiscal framework, no severe imbalances, sustainability) are exactly the ones France is furthest from. Whether the Governing Council would treat a post-election OAT sell-off as "unwarranted, disorderly dynamics" is the single most important unknown in the Le Pen cases, and the AFT will not answer it, but its view of what the ECB has told the Trésor is worth having.

Non-residents hold 57.5%, the highest share among the G7, about half of them in the euro area. The non-euro half is the rate-sensitive part: the Japanese sold more than €20bn of OATs between June and November 2024 and about €30bn over the year as the yen carry trade lost its logic ext; the same group is now the swing buyer when OATs yield more than BTPs. The OAT–BTP 10-year spread inverted in August 2026 (OATs 5 bp over BTPs at one point, France the highest-yielding sovereign in the euro area) ext. For a holder of our size the relevant question is not whether non-residents sell in a shock but whether they are still there to buy the €350–450bn of gross issuance a year that Figure 15 implies.

French insurers and banks (9.6% and 10.5%) are the domestic backstop, and both have limits. Life insurers took a record €44bn of net inflows in 2025 ext, largely into euro funds that buy OATs; those inflows are a function of the OAT yield versus regulated savings (Livret A) and reverse when policy rates rise or when a wealth-tax or assurance-vie tax proposal appears in a budget, which is a live possibility in either 2027 outcome. Their sovereign book is only about 19% of assets after look-through, so the capacity to add is finite. Banks hold OATs largely at amortised cost for liquidity purposes and are therefore insensitive to mark-to-market but sensitive to the sovereign-bank loop through ratings and repo haircuts. Neither group is the buyer of a 2027 supply surge; that is the point of the €{{dur_loss100}}bn figure: it is mostly on the books of holders who can and do leave.

To ask the AFT on holders

  1. Where does the AFT see the marginal buyer of 2027 OAT supply, and how much of the Eurosystem run-off is being absorbed by domestic banks' liquidity books versus non-residents?
  2. Has the Trésor had any indication of how the ECB would read the TPI conditions for France while the EDP is open and off-path?
  3. Does the AFT track the country split of non-resident holders (it does not publish it), and what happened to the Japanese and Asian central-bank bid during the September 2026 widening?

5The 2027 map: Le Pen, Philippe, majority or hung

The first round is on 18 April 2027 and the second on 2 May; legislative elections follow in June. Marine Le Pen is eligible: the Paris Court of Appeal on 7 July 2026 upheld the conviction but cut the ineligibility to 45 months with 30 suspended, and the firm part had already run ext. The polling average in September has Bardella and Le Pen at 34% and 33.5% in the first round and Philippe at 17.5% when he runs without Attal ext. The four configurations the committee asked for are therefore not symmetric probabilities; they are the two candidates most likely to face each other and the two Assembly outcomes each could get. The scenario assumptions are declared in full in section 8; the short version:

Philippe, majority
Pension age towards 65 phased from 2028, spending review; primary balance 1.0 pp of GDP better than the current path by 2031; spread to 45 bp; issuance mix unchanged.
Philippe, hung
Budgets by 49.3 with concessions; primary balance 0.4 pp worse than the current path by 2031; spread stays at 75–80 bp.
Le Pen, hung
Paralysis, not programme: no consolidation and partial giveaways, primary balance 1.0 pp worse by 2030; spread 100 → 120 bp; bills +€25bn, tenor mix one year shorter.
Le Pen, majority
VAT on energy to 5.5% (≈0.5% of GDP), 2023 pension reform rolled back (≈0.5–0.8% of GDP by 2030), partial offsets: primary balance 1.5 pp worse by 2029; spread 110 → 150 → 130 bp; bills +€55bn, tenor mix two years shorter.
Le Pen, rupture (stress)
Open conflict with the Commission over the EDP, TPI in doubt: primary balance 2.0 pp worse, spread 250 bp in 2028, bills +€105bn, tenor mix three years shorter. A stress, not a central case.

All scenarios add a feedback of 3 bp of spread per point of GDP by which the debt ratio exceeds the current path. The Bund curve is the same everywhere. Growth is the same everywhere, which flatters the Le Pen cases (the RN programme is expansionary in year one) and the Philippe majority (consolidation costs growth in year one); neither effect changes the ordering.

Reading the map as a holder

Three things follow. First, the distribution of outcomes for the interest bill is skewed: the best case saves €{{pm_2035_gap_abs}}bn a year by 2035 against the current path, the two Le Pen cases cost €{{lph_2035_gap}}–{{lp_2035_gap}}bn and the stress €{{rup_2035_gap}}bn. A position sized on the current path is short a put on French politics. Second, the hung-Assembly outcomes are closer to the current path than to their majority counterparts on interest, but not on issuance: a hung Assembly under either president means budgets by 49.3, no consolidation, and a spread that stays where 2025–2026 put it. The market has already priced "hung", which is why 85–90 bp is the current level; it has not priced "Le Pen majority", which the engine puts at 150 bp on the 2012 precedent. Third, the Le Pen-majority case is not a solvency case on this arithmetic: debt at {{lp_2035_debt}}% of GDP in 2035 with interest at {{lp_2035_pct}}% is Italy in 2012, not Greece. It becomes one only if the pricing loop runs faster than the fiscal loop, which is a question about the ECB and the marginal buyer, not about French arithmetic. That is why the questions in section 7 are about exits, not about levels.

6Other things that matter in the room

Ratings and the crossover. France is A+ stable at S&P (October 2025) and Fitch (September 2025), AA at DBRS, Aa3 negative at Moody's and AA- negative at Scope ext. Italy is on an upgrade path. A Moody's downgrade to A1 would put France at single-A at three of the four agencies that matter for index weights and repo schedules, and the OAT–BTP inversion of August 2026 says the market has already priced that crossover; some analysts have the OAT–Bund spread at 105 bp by end-Q1 2027 on it ext. Worth asking the AFT what feedback it has had from index providers and from the ECB collateral desk on the single-A question.

The EDP and the Commission. The procedure opened in July 2024; the January 2025 Council recommendation caps net expenditure growth at 0.8% (2025) and 1.2% (2026–2028) with correction by 2029, and the Commission's spring 2026 forecast (deficit 5.1% in 2026, debt 118.1% at end-2026) has France off the path ext. No scenario in section 5 corrects by 2029. The interaction with the TPI conditions is the reason this is more than a compliance question.

The PLF 2027. The 2026 budget passed on 2 February 2026 by 49.3 after two censure motions failed, at a 5.0% deficit (from 4.6% planned) with concessions to the PS and €6.5bn more for defence ext. The PLF 2027 is presented within weeks and is the last budget before the election: the AFT's 2027 financing programme (published in December) will be built on it. The AFT's Chief Economist will know the assumed 2027 deficit and the debt-service line; they are the numbers to get.

Data the AFT could give and does not. Redbox's French chains are blocked at step A because the État's own cash interest and financing tables (PLF programme 117, the tableau de financement) are not machine-readable to us (OQ-8), and the register is gross of buybacks because rachats are published only in aggregate. Per-line buyback volumes and the programme-117 tables would close the French interest chain to the euro. A request in the room costs nothing.

The CADES and the social perimeter. Eurostat's S.1311 securities run €{{eurostat_minus_aft}}bn above the AFT's État total at end-2025 (Figure 1): CADES and the other central bodies. CADES' amortisation schedule (to 2033) is a hidden source of negotiable-debt demand replacement in the late 2020s, and a Le Pen pension rollback would reopen the question of who funds the social deficit.

7Questions to put to the AFT

  1. What interest charge and average cost of the stock does the PLF 2027 assume for 2027 and 2028, and at what 10-year OAT and what spread? (Our register-based current path: €{{cur_2027_bn}}bn and €{{cur_2028_bn}}bn of general-government interest.)
  2. Of the €{{redemptions_2027_eur_bn}}bn the register shows maturing in 2027, how much has already been bought back, and will the AFT publish rachats by line?
  3. Is the 2026 fall in maturity at issue (10.3 years against 12.6 in 2021) a demand observation or a policy, and is there an average-maturity floor?
  4. Under a spread shock, is the bill stock the shock absorber or is there a ceiling? What is the 2027 BTF target?
  5. Who is the marginal buyer of 2027 supply given €110–120bn a year of Eurosystem run-off, and how much of that run-off is domestic bank liquidity books versus non-residents?
  6. Has the Trésor had any indication of how the ECB reads the TPI conditions for a country in an open, off-path EDP?
  7. What has the AFT heard from index providers and the ECB collateral desk on the single-A question, and did the August 2026 OAT–BTP inversion change the investor base?
  8. What happened to the non-euro (Japanese, Asian official) bid in the September 2026 widening, and does the AFT track the country split it does not publish?
  9. Would the AFT consider a formal spread-feedback assumption in its debt-service projections, as the Cour des comptes has urged?
  10. Are the linker programme (8% of the stock, 11% uplifted) and the green OAT programme being scaled back as the long end thins?
  11. If a 2027 government asked for a longer average maturity to lock in rates before a spread shock, what would the AFT say about the cost, and has it been asked?
  12. Will the État's programme-117 tables and the tableau de financement be published in a machine-readable form?

Caveats

  • The register is gross of buybacks on the fixed lines (1–4% above the AFT); redemptions and gross issuance in the engine are correspondingly high. Net issuance and interest are barely affected.
  • Interest is on the accrual basis and general government; the État's budgetary "charge de la dette" (about €58bn in 2026 ext) is a narrower, cash concept and will always be lower.
  • Growth, inflation and the Bund curve are common to all scenarios; the engine is an arithmetic of the register, not a macro model. A recession or an ECB easing cycle would move every line.
  • The strict file's 2025 interest is an AMECO-stitched value (grade B); the Eurostat outturn arrives in October 2026.
  • Holder shares and every political and market fact marked ext are unverified against primary sources; the sources are listed below.
